ABOUT US

VISION

Our Vision is to build AI-Ready → AI-Enabled → AI-Native Schools, where AI becomes an integrated capability across teaching, learning, operations and decision-making.

OUR IDENTITY

We are the AI Transformation Partner for schools built on

  • Deep expertise in AI transformation, technology and emerging AI capabilities.
  • Understanding of pedagogy, curriculum, teachers and the realities of school environments

We empower schools beyond introducing AI tools !

OUR PRINCIPLES

  • School-First, Not Tool-First : Start with the school's goals, curriculum, people and workflows—not with a technology.
  • Augment, Never Replace : AI should amplify human capability, especially the expertise and judgement of educators.
  • Outcomes Over Adoption : Measure success by time saved, teaching effectiveness, learning impact and institutional value not simply AI usage.
  • Responsible by Design : Build AI adoption around privacy, safety, transparency, ethics and appropriate governance.
  • Practical Before Perfect : Focus on real workflows and measurable improvements that schools can implement today.
  • Built for Scale : Create systems, skills and infrastructure that evolve as AI, pedagogy and school needs change.

ASSESSMENT COPILOT

SAMPLE 01

Curriculum-grounded MCQ, short-answer and logical-reasoning questions, with answer keys and rubrics, drafted in minutes instead of hours.

01

PURPOSE

THE CHALLENGE

  • A good assessment takes hours: questions, answer keys, rubrics, and balancing difficulty across the set.
  • Generic AI tools produce questions that drift off-syllabus, miss the grade level, or can't be traced to a learning objective.
  • Higher-order questions are the hardest and slowest to write, so they're often under-used.

OUR PURPOSE

  • Grounded in your curriculum: every question traces back to a specific standard, objective or chapter.
  • Right level, every time: matched to grade, Bloom's level and the difficulty mix the teacher asks for.
  • Teacher in control: the copilot drafts and explains; the teacher reviews and decides.
02

WHAT IT DOES

Multiple Choice

Fast, objective checks of understanding and application.

  • Distractors built from real student misconceptions
  • Rationale for the answer and for every wrong option
  • Balanced answer positions, no giveaway wording

Short Answer

Students explain, justify or calculate in their own words.

  • Command words matched to cognitive level
  • Model answer written at grade level
  • Point-by-point rubric with accept / don't accept lists

Logical Reasoning

Multi-step thinking applied to new situations.

  • Scenarios, claim–evidence, error analysis, Assertion–Reason
  • Expected reasoning chain, step by step
  • Partial-credit rubric for each step, not just the final answer
BriefGrade, board, chapter, question mix
BlueprintObjective, Bloom's level and marks for each question
DraftQuestions, answer key, rubrics
Self-checkAccuracy, clarity, bias and coverage review
Teacher reviewEdit, harder or easier, version B, export
Sample output · Logical ReasoningGrade 7CBSE Science · Heat4 marks

Riya puts one steel spoon and one wooden spoon into the same cup of hot tea. After two minutes she touches the ends of both handles. Riya claims: "The steel spoon got hotter because steel is heavier than wood."

  1. (a) Which handle will feel hotter? (1)
  2. (b) Is Riya's reason correct? Explain using the idea of conduction. (2)
  3. (c) Suggest which material a cooking pan handle should be made of, and why. (1)
Show teacher answer key

Reasoning chain: 1) Heat moves from the tea into the spoons by conduction. 2) Metals are good conductors and wood is a poor conductor. 3) So the steel handle gets hotter, because of conductivity, not weight. 4) A pan handle should therefore be an insulator.

Rubric: (a) steel, 1 mark · (b) rejects the weight reason, 1 mark; correct conduction explanation, 1 mark · (c) names an insulator such as wood or plastic and gives a reason, 1 mark.

Source: Ch. "Heat", section on conduction; good and poor conductors.

GRADING ASSISTANT

SAMPLE 02

Rubric-based marks backed by evidence from every answer, with feedback students can act on and a class view of misconceptions, all approved by the teacher.

01

PURPOSE

THE CHALLENGE

  • Marking written answers is slow and repetitive, and it eats into evenings and weekends.
  • Feedback gets shorter as the pile gets taller, so students receive a mark instead of a next step.
  • Class-wide misconceptions stay hidden inside individual scripts, so reteaching is guesswork.

OUR PURPOSE

  • Evidence or zero: marks are awarded only when the student's own words can be quoted to earn them.
  • Consistent and fair: the same rubric decision rules for every student, with uncertain cases flagged for the teacher.
  • Teacher in control: every mark is a suggestion until the teacher approves it.
02

WHAT IT DOES

Evidence-Backed Marks

Criterion-by-criterion marking against your rubric.

  • Verbatim quote supporting every mark awarded
  • MCQs auto-marked against the answer key
  • Totals double-checked, never estimated

Actionable Feedback

One strength and one next step for every answer.

  • Targets the lowest-scoring criterion
  • Grade-appropriate and encouraging, with no peer comparison
  • Ready to share with students when you approve it

Class Insights

See what the whole class understood and what it missed.

  • Misconceptions ranked, with the students affected
  • Lowest-scoring criteria and a reteach focus
  • Marks export ready for your LMS
Rubric & answersWorks directly with Assessment Copilot rubrics
Input checkMarks add up, IDs anonymised

SELF-STUDY TUTOR

SAMPLE 03

A Socratic tutor that helps students think, not copy: patient, step-by-step guidance grounded in their curriculum, whenever they study.

01

PURPOSE

THE CHALLENGE

  • Students get stuck on homework at home, with no one to ask until the next lesson.
  • General AI chatbots hand over full answers, so students finish the work without learning anything.
  • Teachers can't see where each student struggled, so class time goes on re-explaining.

OUR PURPOSE

  • The student does the thinking: guiding questions and escalating hints, never a shortcut to the answer.
  • Grounded and verified: explanations come from the class curriculum, and every step is checked before it's confirmed.
  • Visible to teachers: a short session summary shows where each student struggled.
02

WHAT IT DOES

Socratic Coaching

One question at a time, matched to where the student is stuck.

  • Five-level hint ladder, raised only when needed
  • Worked examples use a parallel problem, not the student's own
  • Ends each problem by asking the student to explain why the method works

Safe & Honest

Built for students, with guardrails schools can trust.

  • Graded work: guidance only, never solutions
  • Checks every answer before calling it right
  • Safeguarding concerns are escalated to a trusted adult

Teacher Session Summary

What happened in each session, at a glance.

  • Problems attempted and how much help each needed
  • Misconceptions, quoted in the student's own words
  • Mastery signal and the recommended next practice
L0 DiagnoseWhat have you tried so far?
L1 NudgeA focusing question
L2 ConceptNames the rule, doesn't apply it
L3 First stepOnly the first move
L4 Parallel exampleSame method, different numbers
Sample session · Homework helpGrade 7Maths · Linear equationsSolved at L1
STUDENTwhat's x in 3x + 5 = 20
TUTOR · L0Let's work it out together. What is the equation asking you to find?
STUDENTx i guess. idk how

ASSIGNMENT ADVISOR

SAMPLE 04

A writing and project mentor that coaches students from first idea to final check, against their teacher's rubric and in their own voice.

01

PURPOSE

THE CHALLENGE

  • Detailed draft feedback takes 10+ minutes per student, so most students get one round, at the very end.
  • AI writing tools will happily write the essay for them, which undermines learning and integrity.
  • Students leave long assignments to the last minute without a plan.

OUR PURPOSE

  • Coach, never ghostwrite: questions, evidence and next steps, while the words stay the student's own.
  • Rubric-anchored: every comment quotes the draft and maps to the teacher's criteria.
  • Feedback at every stage: from brainstorm to final check, with a plan to hit the deadline.
02

WHAT IT DOES

Evidence-Backed Feedback

Specific, rubric-linked comments on the student's own work.

  • Quotes the exact sentence each comment refers to
  • Top 3 priorities only, ranked by rubric weight
  • A guiding question for each priority

Integrity Built In

Help that keeps the work authentically the student's.

  • Never writes or rewrites the student's text
  • Never invents sources; suggests types of source to look for
  • Logs requests to write the work, for the teacher

Project Planning

Turns a due date into a plan students can follow.

  • Dated milestones from today to the deadline
  • One clear deliverable per milestone
  • Final-check checklist against the brief
BrainstormUnderstand the task, narrow the scope
OutlineStructure and plan for evidence
DraftArgument and evidence against the rubric
RevisionTop 3 priorities
Final checkWord limit, citations, brief requirements
